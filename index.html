How I work

Four habits show up in every project. Each links to the place where I actually did it.

1. Write the plan before the data

Decision, reader, metrics, method and predictions are written down first, so I can't quietly move the goalposts.

Every analysis has a research plan with logged predictions, and the write-up scores them, misses included.

2. Check the data before trusting it

Most wrong answers come from the data, not the model.

An A/B test that failed its own randomization check. Recent loans that look safe only because they are young. A model that scores 0.99 by reading the outcome.

3. Real is not the same as worth acting on

A significant result is not a decision. I convert effects into money, costs and break-even points.

Both test emails work; only one clearly pays. A loan cut-off is set by cost, not accuracy.

4. Say where it breaks

Each project ends with its limits, and the findings are stress-tested before I report them.

Rankings re-run under 10,000 random weightings; conclusions re-run with each outlier app removed.

Banking and financial services

Which applicants should a lender approve, and at what cut-off?

AnswerUse an explainable scorecard and approve the safest 76%. On loans it had never seen, that earns $0.39M more than approving everyone (95% CI $0.18M to $0.59M), and ranks risk better than the platform's own score (AUC 0.711 vs 0.647).
What I caughtCounting young loans as "good" made 2013 loans look risk-free, so I used a fixed 12-month window. A boosted model added nothing measurable (+0.012 AUC, interval includes zero).
  • Credit modeling
  • Right-censoring
  • Calibration
  • Cost-based decisions

Where do scam losses concentrate, and where should a bank add friction?

AnswerA few large losses are most of the money: 12.6% of loss reports hold at least 87.6% of the dollars, and bank transfers and crypto dominate the classified losses. Whether friction pays depends chiefly on who bears the loss: the break-even scam rate falls 16-fold if the bank reimburses.
What I caughtOnly 6.3% of scam complaints to the CFPB ended in monetary relief, and 15 of 29 large companies differ from the pooled rate by more than chance, so I framed the comparison as "differ", not "better".

Which complaint streams should be escalated this week?

AnswerRun a robust, alert-aware rule at about 6 new alerts a week. In streams above 20 complaints a week it catches a 2x surge every time, and I back-tested it against four other rules.
What I caughtAfter a 21,608-complaint wave at Cash App (against about 45 a week), the textbook "mean + SD" rule needed roughly 11,000 complaints to alert, for 26 weeks. It was blind to everything smaller.
  • Monitoring

What should a bank fix first in its mobile app?

AnswerLogin and stability first: 37% of unhappy bank reviews mention them. Frozen or closed accounts are rare at banks (3%) but the costliest theme (odds ratio 12.8). Redesigns draw the most comments but the least rating damage.
What I caughtAverage rating across apps measured how often each app prompts happy users, not quality (0.99 correlation with five-star share), so I compared only 1 and 2 star reviews and validated my topic tagger on 200 unseen reviews.

Experimentation

Which email should a retailer send, and is it worth the cost?

AnswerSend the Men's email: about $0.26 profit per customer. The Women's email is real but marginal ($0.12, and the lower bound is $0.002), so it depends on margin and send cost.
What I caughtChecking a no-effect test every day and stopping at the first win declares a false winner 21% of the time, not 5%. The plan, metrics and decision rule were fixed before looking at outcomes.
  • Randomized test
  • Multiple comparisons
  • Power and peeking

Should a mobile game move its first gate from level 30 to level 40?

AnswerNo. Seven-day retention falls 0.8 points (95% CI 0.3 to 1.3 lower), and one-day retention is inconclusive.
What I caughtThe test fails its own sample-ratio check (p = 0.009): 789 more players landed in one arm. The direction survives the worst case (still 0.66 points lower), but the size is less certain, so I flagged it to find out why.

Product and market research

What should my pregnancy-journal app build first?

AnswerFix trust problems first: reviews that mention ads, updates or bugs average 2.4 to 2.6 stars against 4.16 overall. Journaling is the clearest opening but a thin one. Pricing, updates and ads stayed in the top three of the priority ranking in 84% to 99% of 5,000 random weightings.
What I caughtAn off-the-shelf sentiment model labeled only 44% of 1 and 2 star reviews as negative, so I built and validated topic rules on held-out reviews. Only 7 of 19 topics passed, and only those are treated as measurements.
  • Review analysis
  • Validation
  • Prioritization
  • Own product

Why don't Olist customers come back, and what should Olist fix first?

AnswerTrue retention is about 2%, and delivery barely moves it, but lateness is the main driver of bad reviews. Start with the Rio de Janeiro route, which produces 42% of excess late orders.
What I caught30% of "repeat" orders were split baskets placed the same day, which made retention look like 3% when it is closer to 2%. Fixing delivery pays off in reviews, not in repeat customers.

Where I was wrong

I log predictions before each analysis and score them afterwards. These are the misses. They are the reason the right answers can be trusted.

  • Credit risk. I expected the lending platform's own score to be about as good as my scorecard. Mine ranked risk better by 0.064 AUC.
  • Complaint monitor. I expected a robust rule to recover quickly after a huge spike. It was only partly protected: its threshold sat 3 to 5 times above the new normal until I added an alert-aware baseline.
  • Bank app reviews. I predicted customer service would be the costliest theme. It ranked fifth; frozen or closed accounts ranked first by a wide margin.
  • Email test. I expected adjusting for past spend to tighten the estimates. It removed 0.04% of the variance, so I reported it and did not use it.
  • Scam losses. I predicted the break-even scam rate for bank transfers would be above 1% at today's loss share. At my base assumptions it is 0.72%.
  • Cookie Cats test. The experiment itself failed its randomization check, which a quick analysis would never have noticed.
